\subsection{\structurechange{Multi-Timescale Semantic--Physical Coordination}}
\label{sec:semantic_physical_coordination}

\structurechange{This subsection presents the second coordination component:
when semantic authority should enter or re-enter physical execution that may
evolve faster than semantic reasoning.} The mechanism starts from the versioned bounded
\structurechange{Committed Execution Directive $c_v$} in
Section~\ref{sec:local_execution}, keeps controller and perception state
local between semantic events, and revalidates only the obligations exposed by
an event. Fold II is therefore an operational coordination mechanism,
\structurechange{not merely the execution boundary}: it routes typed events,
constructs their affected scope, arbitrates concurrent updates, and assigns a
disposition to each active \structurechange{directive}.

\subsubsection{\structurechange{Event Transactions}}

\formalchange{The semantic update operator acts only at event boundaries. If
the transaction at $\tau_k$ commits $S^{\mathrm{sem}}_{\nu+1}$ and no further
semantic event occurs before $\tau_{k+1}$, then
$S^{\mathrm{sem}}(t)=S^{\mathrm{sem}}_{\nu+1}$ for
$t\in(\tau_k,\tau_{k+1})$, while $x^{\mathrm{phys}}(t)$ continues to evolve
under directives that retain execution authority and under local guards
according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical_evolution}. Raw controller and perception
samples do not enter $U$ merely because physical time advances.}

\finalchange{At $\tau_k$, the method applies four conceptual stages. First,
role-owned interpretation maps scene records to an Affordance delta $d_A$,
direct task edits to a Planning delta $d_P^{\mathrm{task}}$, and event-level
execution evidence to a candidate Verification delta $d_V$. Records within each
role are ordered by event time and stable event identity and are interpreted
from the same committed state. Second, after applying the compatibility rule
below to any completion component, the admissible, disjoint role-owned deltas
merge atomically into an intermediate state $\bar S_k$, with footprint
$\chi_k=\chi(d_A)\cup\chi(d_P^{\mathrm{task}})\cup\chi(d_V)$; an absent event
type contributes an empty delta. Third, the affected scope is constructed from
that footprint, direct task edits, verification source obligations, and their
dependency consumers. Against $\bar S_k$, the same Planning Role revalidates or
repairs only this scope and only its owned fields. Thus, direct task
interpretation first changes Planning-owned fields, and affected-scope
revalidation uses the combined role-owned changes without introducing a second
planner. Fourth, the resulting state is committed as
$S^{\mathrm{sem}}_{\nu+1}$, after which the method assigns dispositions to
previously active directives and dispatches any new or replacement directive
from the new state rather than mutating an old directive in place.}

\finalchange{A same-batch task edit constrains whether older execution evidence
may certify completion. Let
$\operatorname{compat}_k(c_v)$ mean that the obligation definition obtained
after applying $d_P^{\mathrm{task}}$ retains the target identity, intended
semantic effect, and other task-defining semantic fields authorized by $c_v$ in
its originating state.
A Verification-owned completion update bound to $c_v$ is admissible only when
$v$ is not withdrawn and $\operatorname{compat}_k(c_v)$ holds. Otherwise, the
evidence remains valid execution history and may record what physically
occurred, but it cannot set the revised obligation to complete. This check is
applied before the completion component of $d_V$ enters the intermediate merge,
so disjoint write domains cannot combine a newly edited obligation with stale
completion status. Typed records retain one transaction identifier; role
ownership resolves cross-role writes, and a stable per-role fold resolves
concurrent same-role writes.}

\subsubsection{\structurechange{Affected-Obligation Construction}}

\structurechange{For each obligation $v$, let $\operatorname{req}(v)$ collect
the semantic facts read by its target binding, capability requirements,
preconditions, intended effect, and semantic guards. Let
$\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{edit}}_k$ be the directly edited obligations, and let
$\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{src}}_k$ contain the source obligation named by a
verification event. The Planning Role constructs the direct seed set}
\begin{equation}
\structurechange{\mathcal{B}_k=
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{edit}}_k\cup\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{src}}_k
\cup\{v:\operatorname{req}(v)\cap\chi_k\neq\varnothing\}.}
\label{eq:affected_seed}
\end{equation}
\formalchange{Equation~\eqref{eq:affected_seed} combines the three direct
sources of affected work.} \structurechange{It then computes the
dependency-consumer closure}
\begin{equation}
\structurechange{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{aff}}_k=
\mathcal{B}_k\cup
\operatorname{Reach}^{+}_{G^-\cup G^+}(\mathcal{B}_k),}
\label{eq:affected_set}
\end{equation}
\structurechange{where $G^-$ and $G^+$ are the graphs before and after the
\formalchange{direct task edit}. Their union preserves dependency paths that
the \formalchange{edit} adds or removes.} \formalchange{The operator
$\operatorname{Reach}^{+}_{G^-\cup G^+}(\mathcal B_k)$ follows edges from each
seed to downstream obligations that consume its semantic effect.}
\structurechange{Scene events seed obligations that read changed identity,
affordance, or capability facts; task events seed added, withdrawn, or modified
obligations; and verification events seed the evidenced obligation.
Equation~\eqref{eq:affected_set} also includes consumers of their semantic
effects. This is a conservative causal scope, not an optimal or globally
minimal repair claim. The Planning Role owns this construction because it owns
$G$ and assignment, but it cannot alter the Affordance or Verification deltas
that supplied the footprint. Any repair is evaluated against $\bar S_k$ and
committed only to Planning-owned fields before the transaction publishes its
final version.}

\subsubsection{\structurechange{Directive Dispositions and Selective Semantic Re-Entry}}

\formalchange{Let $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{act}}_k=\{c_v\mid c_v\text{ retains
execution authority at }\tau_k\}$ denote the active committed directives
visible at the event boundary. The bounded execution context supplied to the
disposition stage is}
\begin{equation}
\finalchange{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{exec}}_k=
\{(c_v,\sigma^{\mathrm{guard}}_{v,k})
  \mid c_v\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{act}}_k\},}
\label{eq:execution_context}
\end{equation}
\finalchange{where $\sigma^{\mathrm{guard}}_{v,k}$ is a bounded local
admissibility certificate for execution and safety guards, not raw physical
state. Equation~\eqref{eq:execution_context} contains active directive
identities and bounded guard certificates; each originating version $\nu_v$ is
read from the corresponding $c_v$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:committed_directive}. The
context contains no trajectory, controller state, tracking state, servo target,
per-tick target, low-level gain, or raw sensor stream. Event-level evidence is
carried by $\mathbf e_k$, so the complete event update is}
\begin{equation}
\formalchange{U(S^{\mathrm{sem}}_{\nu},\mathbf{e}_k,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)
=\left(S^{\mathrm{sem}}_{\nu+1},
       \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{aff}}_k,D_k\right).}
\label{eq:semantic_update}
\end{equation}
\formalchange{The semantic delta merge in Eq.~\eqref{eq:semantic_update}
depends on $S^{\mathrm{sem}}_{\nu}$ and $\mathbf e_k$; the bounded execution
context is consulted only to assign dispositions to existing commitments. It
does not grant the semantic layer access to controller-rate state.}

\formalchange{For every active committed directive, the transaction records}
\begin{equation}
\formalchange{D_k:\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{act}}_k\rightarrow
\{\textsc{complete},\textsc{continue},
  \textsc{hold},\textsc{revoke}\}.}
\label{eq:directive_disposition}
\end{equation}
\formalchange{Equation~\eqref{eq:directive_disposition} acts on execution
authority. In contrast, $z$ is the Verification-owned semantic status of an
obligation: it records whether the intended effect is verified, semantically
complete, failed, or requires reconsideration. The map $D_k$ neither writes nor
duplicates $z$ and is not a workflow scheduler; at $\tau_k$, it answers whether
each existing directive may continue, must enter a bounded hold, loses
authority, or is retired after verified completion. The transaction derives
$D_k$ from role-owned semantic fields, the originating version $\nu_v$, and the
bounded guard certificate.}

\finalchange{The disposition priority is deterministic. A withdrawn
obligation, or a directive that conflicts with the final target, assignment,
skill, or semantic guards, is \textsc{revoke}. An unchanged intended effect
accepted by the Verification Role from evidence bound to $c_v$ and $\nu_v$ is
\textsc{complete} only if $\operatorname{compat}_k(c_v)$ holds. A
directive whose authorizing semantic fields and guards remain valid is
\textsc{continue}. An unresolved affected directive is \textsc{hold} only when
a bounded safe local hold remains admissible; otherwise it is \textsc{revoke}.
Actuator-local and team resource or safety guards are non-overridable, so a
failed guard prevents continuation even when semantic fields are unchanged.}

\formalchange{These outcomes define the post-update directive lifecycle.
\textsc{Continue} retains the existing $c_v$ without changing its originating
version $\nu_v$, even though the authoritative state has advanced to
$S^{\mathrm{sem}}_{\nu+1}$; continuation states that the fields authorizing that
directive remain valid under the new state. \textsc{Hold} retains the directive
identity but temporarily bounds execution authority at a safe local hold; it
does not complete, reassign, retarget, or recommit the obligation.
\textsc{Revoke} removes the old directive's execution authority and retires it
to execution history. \textsc{Complete} retires the originating directive only
after Verification accepts its intended semantic effect under the current
semantic interpretation and records the corresponding status in $z$. If
Planning changes the obligation definition, target, assignment, bounded skill,
or relevant semantic guard, the old directive is never mutated in place;
Planning may instead issue a new or replacement directive $c'_v$ from the newly
committed version $\nu+1$ according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:committed_directive}.}

\formalchange{A directive $c_v\in\mathcal D_k^{\mathrm{act}}$ whose obligation
lies outside $\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{aff}}_k$ receives \textsc{continue} by
default when its authorizing semantic fields and local guards remain valid.}
An obligation inside the set is
not automatically cancelled: revalidation may preserve it, while changed or
unresolved commitments cause hold or revocation. Large-model reasoning can
therefore re-enter only through the role whose semantic evidence or authority is
needed. A scene event reactivates Affordance interpretation; a task edit or an
invalidated plan field reactivates Planning; completion or failure evidence
reactivates Verification. The graph closure and unchanged-field checks remain
deterministic coordination operations and do not require controller-rate model
calls.

This mechanism does not assume that minimal semantic intervention or maximal
continuation is always preferable. It fixes which obligations must be examined,
which role may change each semantic field, and which bounded dispositions are
available. \localexecchange{The final subsection explains the complementary
between-event operation: how an unchanged directive governs vision-driven
actuator-local skill execution while its physical realization continues to
evolve.}

\subsection{\localexecchange{Directive-Governed Actuator-Local Execution}}
\label{sec:actuator_local_execution}

\localexecchange{Section~\ref{sec:role_shared_coordination} defines and
authorizes $c_v$, while Section~\ref{sec:semantic_physical_coordination}
determines its disposition at semantic event boundaries. Between those
boundaries, the assigned actuator consumes $c_v$ by executing the selected
public skill $\kappa_v$ from current policy-visible RGB or RGB-D observations
and conventional actuator-local closed-loop control.}

\begin{equation}
\localexecchange{u_i(t)=\pi_i\!\left(o_i^{\mathrm{vis}}(t),c_v\right),}
\label{eq:local_skill_realization}
\end{equation}
\localexecchange{Equation~\eqref{eq:local_skill_realization} expresses this
realization without prescribing a controller. Here, $u_i(t)$ is the actuator
command used in Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical_evolution}, $\pi_i$ is the
actuator-local realization of the public skill authorized by $\kappa_v$, and
$o_i^{\mathrm{vis}}(t)$ collects current policy-visible RGB or RGB-D
measurements, visual tracking and image-space errors, depth-derived geometry,
and physically available robot-local state. The input $c_v$ is the semantic
commitment in Eq.~\eqref{eq:committed_directive}. Thus, the semantic layer fixes
the obligation, actuator, and skill, whereas local execution determines their
physical realization. In particular, $c_v\neq u_i(t)$: a directive is not a
trajectory, control command, or low-level actuator reference.}

\localexecchange{The directive fixes the obligation, semantic target, assigned
actuator, public skill, guards, originating version, and intended effect. Local
execution may instead update visual estimates and errors, tracking and
grasp-relative state, trajectory and servo correction, waiting or reacquisition,
contact observations, and controller state. It may change how $\kappa_v$ is
physically realized, but it may not rebind $q_v$, substitute another public
skill, transfer the obligation, or write semantic status $z$. Any such semantic
change requires re-entry through III-B.}

\localexecchange{The local skill continuously enforces its execution, resource,
and safety conditions. At $\tau_k$, it summarizes the relevant locally
observable validity as the bounded certificate
$\sigma^{\mathrm{guard}}_{v,k}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:execution_context}. The semantic layer receives this certificate
and bounded event-level evidence, not raw images, per-frame visual errors,
trajectories, servo commands, low-level gains, or complete tracking and
controller state. Local execution is vision-driven: it does not consume
simulator ground-truth object pose or velocity, commanded conveyor velocity,
future target trajectories, hidden physics state, or oracle reachability.
Target motion, when required, is estimated visually from successive
observations.}

\localexecchange{Moving-PICK is one representative public-skill realization,
not a method component or contribution. For a dynamic-target PICK directive,
$q_v$ and $a(v)$ remain fixed while current RGB-D observations update the
physical target estimate and target-relative execution state. Visual tracking,
capture waiting, target-relative correction, and bounded reacquisition operate
locally using established mechanisms~\cite{cuevas2018hybrid,
haviland2020finalphase,liu2023target,maranci2026reactive}. Temporary visual loss
may cause a bounded local wait or reacquisition; persistent loss, ambiguity,
identity inconsistency, or failed physical interaction produces event-level
evidence. No semantic reasoning is invoked at every perception or control
step.}

\begin{equation}
\localexecchange{y_i^{\mathrm{exec}}(t)
=\phi_i\!\left(o_i^{\mathrm{vis}}(t),u_i(t)\right),}
\label{eq:execution_evidence}
\end{equation}
\localexecchange{In Eq.~\eqref{eq:execution_evidence}, $\phi_i$ is a local
evidence-extraction map, not a semantic verifier. Its bounded output reports
completion, failure, ambiguity, visual loss, contact rejection, or persistent
local infeasibility under the active directive. The return path is
$y_i^{\mathrm{exec}}\rightarrow\mathbf e_k\rightarrow
U(S^{\mathrm{sem}}_{\nu},\mathbf e_k,\mathcal C^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:semantic_update}. A verification event carries the evidence to
the Verification Role, which may update $z$ only when it remains semantically
compatible; III-B determines $D_k$. The local execution layer assigns neither
quantity. This realizes the physical half of Multi-Timescale Semantic--Physical
Coordination without creating a third contribution.}
