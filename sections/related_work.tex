\subsection{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\providecommand{\localexecchange}[1]{#1}

Language-guided manipulation systems use large models to interpret instructions,
ground objects and skills, and connect semantic plans to executable actions
through feedback, scene graphs, or spatial constraints~\cite{huang2023inner,
liang2023code,rana2023sayplan,huang2023voxposer}. For multiple robots, RoCo
assigns a language-model agent to each robot, SMART-LLM forms capability-aware
coalitions, COHERENT alternates centralized proposals with robot feedback, and
LiP-LLM combines dependencies with optimization-based allocation~\cite{mandi2024roco,
kannan2024smartllm,liu2025coherent,obata2025lipllm}. These systems establish
semantic decomposition, capability grounding, and feedback-driven coordination.
Our focus is the organization of semantic responsibility and authority over a
heterogeneous multi-arm team, rather than a new model-internal reasoning
procedure.

He et al.~\cite{he2026closedloop} provide the closest architectural comparison.
Their disclosed hierarchy contains a Planning Agent, a Manipulation Agent
associated with each robot, and an associated Verification Agent, together with
semantic sub-operations, visual/action tools, and feedback-driven recovery.
Both papers therefore connect semantic planning with physical execution and
verification. The present paper studies team-level Affordance, Planning, and
Verification Roles over authoritative semantic state, followed by
manipulator-local execution and versioned event re-entry. The comparison is
structural: properties not specified in the public description of He et al.,
such as shared state or scheduling semantics, are not treated as absent.

Dynamic manipulation has established solutions for tracked interception,
reactive grasp refinement, and moving-conveyor operation~\cite{marturi2019dynamic,
morrison2018closing,maranci2026reactive}. Target-referenced grasping maintains
correspondence to a selected object across observations~\cite{liu2023target};
hybrid global and eye-in-hand sensing and final-phase image-based servoing are
also established~\cite{cuevas2018hybrid,haviland2020finalphase}. Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2025adaptivegrasp} combine global-to-local observation with
static-to-dynamic planning for moving clutter. \localexecchange{These
established mechanisms serve as physical ingredients within vision-driven
manipulator-local skills; they do not define a standalone method component and
are not claimed independently.}

Execution monitoring, continual planning, behavior-tree replanning, and
feedback-driven recovery address runtime deviations~\cite{pettersson2005execution,
brenner2009continual,levihn2013foresight,pezzato2023active}. Language-guided
systems likewise use perceptual feedback, plan verification, or dedicated
verification agents to revise semantic action sequences~\cite{huang2023inner,
rana2023sayplan,he2026closedloop}. The remaining question is how such semantic
events interact with physical work that may remain valid and continue locally.
The present method makes this interaction explicit: scene, task, and
verification events are routed through role-specific responsibilities, the
affected subtask scope is constructed from semantic dependencies, and valid
unaffected execution continues whenever the revised state permits it.
